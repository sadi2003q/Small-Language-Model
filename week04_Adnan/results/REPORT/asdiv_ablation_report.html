Gain vs Question Count — Base vs Fine-tuned Comparison

Fine-tuned guide gains on Multi-step (+16.7%); base guide loses (−3.3%) — critical reversal

Finding: The base 3B guide helps only on Division (+12.5%) and Multiplication (+6.2%) — operations where a simple structural hint (e.g. "divide X by Y") is correct even without fine-tuning. However, Multi-step problems — the most common category at 30 questions — are hurt by −3.3 pts, and both Addition (−7.7%) and Subtraction (−8.0%) also regress. The fine-tuned guide's strongest result was Multi-step (+16.7%); the base guide's worst result is also Multi-step. This inversion isolates exactly what LoRA training learned: how to decompose compound reasoning chains.

Summary: The pipeline architecture alone (base 3B guide → 1.5B solver × 5) produces results that are statistically identical to or slightly worse than the unguided baseline. Every positive result in the fine-tuned report — the +11 pt accuracy gain, 1.19× vote consistency lift, 53.2% ECE improvement, and Multi-step specialisation — is attributable to LoRA fine-tuning of the guide model. The base guide's plans introduce noise on Addition, Subtraction, and Multi-step problems while coincidentally helping on Division, suggesting the base model lacks the structured decomposition skill that fine-tuning explicitly instilled.
