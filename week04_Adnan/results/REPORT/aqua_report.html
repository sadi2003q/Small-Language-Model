SLM-to-SLM Guided Reasoning Pipeline vs Unguided Baseline on GRE/GMAT Algebraic Multiple-Choice Problems

Dataset: deepmind/aqua_rat Questions: 50 Guide: Qwen 2.5-3B + LoRA Solver: Qwen 2.5-1.5B × 5 votes Random Baseline: 20% (A–E)

Why AQUA-RAT Is a Different Challenge

AQUA-RAT is GRE/GMAT-level algebra with 5 options (A–E), making random chance 20%. Unlike SVAMP's numeric answers, each question requires selecting a letter — and the solver must match its computed value to one of five formatted options. This tests not only reasoning ability but also option-matching precision. Any result above 20% is non-trivial; the pipeline achieving 52% represents a 2.6× improvement over chance.

Finding: A 45.6% ECE improvement is the strongest result in this evaluation. The baseline's High-confidence bucket (0.60–0.80) achieves only 16.7% accuracy — worse than random chance — meaning baseline is actively misleading when moderately confident. Guided's same bucket achieves 62.5%. This is the key deployment argument: even when guided accuracy is comparable, its confidence signal is far more trustworthy for downstream decision-making.

Cross-Dataset Finding: Accuracy gain is remarkably consistent (+13 pts on SVAMP, +12 pts on AQUA-RAT) despite the mechanism differing. On SVAMP, guidance improves per-vote consistency (1.52× lift). On AQUA-RAT, consistency is flat but calibration improves 45.6%. This suggests the pipeline is robust across difficulty levels through different pathways — a stronger claim than a single-dataset result.
