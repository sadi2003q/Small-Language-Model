§ 07Key Findings
🔬
Fine-Tuning Is the Mechanism, Not Size
Across all fair comparisons (same temp), the fine-tuned guide outperforms the base guide by +8–11 pts. A 3B model without LoRA contributes near-zero to accuracy in 3 of 4 datasets. Planning structure, not model size, is what propagates through the solver ensemble.
⚠️
Base Guide Actively Harms Out-of-Domain
On CommonsenseQA (N=250), the base guide produces −2 pts accuracy and 22% worse calibration. It adds 11 false-confidence cases. Unstructured commonsense plans mislead the solver into unanimous wrong answers — worse than no guidance at all.
📐
SVAMP Confound: Read Carefully
The SVAMP ablation used solver temp 0.7 (vs 0.4 everywhere else), weakening the baseline to 48% and inflating the base guide's delta to +15 pts. In absolute terms, the fine-tuned guide still wins: 68% vs 63%. The +15 pt delta is misleading without this context.
🧠
Two Separable Mechanisms
ARC-Challenge reveals both layers: Layer 1 — Factual Knowledge (size): +2 pts from broader 3B science recall. Layer 2 — Planning Structure (fine-tuning): +5 more pts from step-by-step decomposition. Fine-tuning accounts for ~71% of the total gain even on the dataset most favourable to size.
📊
Calibration Gap Is Decisive
Fine-tuning improves ECE by 22–53% across all datasets. The base guide improves ECE in only 2 of 4 datasets, and worsens it in 2. The ECE gap is the clearest single separator between conditions — more reliable than accuracy alone.
🔁
Refiner Rate Reveals Plan Quality
On SVAMP, the base guide triggered the refiner 14 times (vs near-zero for fine-tuned), resolving correctly only 3 times (21%). Unstructured plans leave solver votes split; fine-tuned structured plans produce tight consensus with almost no tie-breaking needed.

🏁 Conclusion

  • Fine-tuning (LoRA) is the primary and decisive mechanism. The pipeline's accuracy gains are not explained by model size alone.
  • The base 3B guide adds small knowledge-level gains on science (+2 pts, ARC) but fails on math without planning (+0/−1 pts, ASDiv) and actively harms out-of-domain tasks (−2 pts, CSQA).
  • Fine-tuned vs base guide gap: +8 pts (ASDiv), +5 pts (ARC), +11 pts (CSQA) — consistent across domains at identical 10.5B compute cost.
  • Calibration is always better with fine-tuning. ECE improvement ranges 22–53%. The base guide degrades calibration in 2 of 4 datasets, creating dangerous false confidence.
  • The SVAMP confound must be disclosed. The +15 pt base guide delta was measured at temp 0.7 vs the fine-tuned guide's temp 0.4 baseline. Absolute accuracy still favours fine-tuning (68% vs 63%).
  • Deployment recommendation: Never deploy the base guide as a substitute for the fine-tuned guide. On out-of-domain tasks, the base guide is worse than no guide at all.
