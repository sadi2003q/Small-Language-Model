§ 01 — Experimental Setup

What Changed. What Stayed Identical.

The only variable between the base and fine-tuned conditions is whether the guide model carries a LoRA adapter trained on GSM8K math. Everything else — the solver model, the number of votes, the temperatures, the dataset, the seed, the compute cost — is held constant. This isolates fine-tuning as the causal variable.

Important: The baseline accuracy differs slightly between this notebook (72.0%) and the original fine-tuned run (70.0%). Same seed 42, same questions — but solver inference at temperature 0.4 is stochastic, producing ~2 pt natural variance across independent runs at N=100. This is expected and normal. § 05 — Mechanism Analysis

Two Layers: Knowledge and Planning Structure

This ablation reveals that guidance has two separable components. The base 3B model contributes one of them. Fine-tuning adds the other. Understanding which layer does what explains the results across both ASDiv and ARC-Challenge.

🧠 Layer 1: Factual Knowledge (Size)
A 3B model has seen more training data than a 1.5B model and has broader factual recall — especially relevant on science questions. When the base guide produces an output about a science question, it may correctly identify relevant concepts (endothermic reactions, plate tectonics, cell division) even without structured planning. This knowledge transfer is what produces the +2.0 pt gain on ARC-Challenge from the base guide, compared to −1.0 pt on ASDiv where knowledge isn't the bottleneck — planning structure is.
+2.0 pts
Contribution on ARC-Challenge (base 3B, no LoRA)
📋 Layer 2: Planning Structure (Fine-Tuning)
GSM8K fine-tuning teaches the model to produce explicit, enumerated reasoning steps — identify what is being asked, apply the relevant principle, eliminate wrong options, commit to an answer with justification. This structured format propagates through to solver votes in two ways: it anchors the solver to the correct reasoning path (accuracy), and it creates tight unanimous vote clusters instead of scattered 3-2 splits (calibration). This layer is what the base model lacks — and it accounts for the remaining +5 pts and 3.6× ECE advantage over the base guide.

ASDiv vs ARC — Base 3B Performance

On math (ASDiv), the base 3B guide hurts (−1 pt) — no knowledge advantage, no planning structure. On science (ARC), it helps (+2 pts) — knowledge transfers but planning does not. Fine-tuning adds planning structure in both cases.

What Each Component Contributes on ARC-Challenge

Decomposing the +7 pt total gain into knowledge (from size) and planning structure (from fine-tuning). Fine-tuning accounts for ~71% of the total gain even on the dataset most favourable to size.
The revised claim — more precise and more defensible: Fine-tuning is the primary mechanism in both math and science. On math, a base 3B guide adds zero value (−1 pt, ASDiv) — planning structure is everything. On science, a base 3B guide adds a small positive value (+2 pts, ARC) from factual knowledge — but fine-tuning multiplies this into 3.5× larger gains and 3.6× better calibration by adding the critical planning structure on top. Across both datasets, the fine-tuned guide outperforms the base guide by 8–12 percentage points at identical compute. Size alone is insufficient; fine-tuned planning structure is what makes the pipeline work.

What the ARC-Challenge Ablation Reveals

The base 3B guide adds a genuine but modest +2.0 pt gain on ARC-Challenge science questions — unlike ASDiv math where it produced −1.0 pts. This difference is meaningful: a 3B model brings broader factual knowledge that partially transfers to science reasoning even without structured planning. However, fine-tuning converts this small knowledge advantage into a 3.5× larger accuracy gain and a 3.6× greater calibration improvement at identical 10.5B param-pass cost. The two-dataset ablation together establishes a clear and consistent picture: model size contributes domain-specific knowledge where available, but fine-tuned planning structure is the primary mechanism that drives accuracy, consistency, and calibration improvements across the pipeline.
