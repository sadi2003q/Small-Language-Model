On Wasted Votes
Guided wastes more votes (41 vs 22) — a reversal from other datasets. This is a direct consequence of bias correction. The baseline almost always picks A (93.4%), so its votes are artificially unanimous even when wrong. Once the guide distributes votes more evenly across A and B, some questions that were wrongly unanimous become correctly split. The guide trades false unanimity for genuine reasoning.

The Headline Finding — A-Option Collapse
The unguided 1.5B solver picks option A on 93.4% of PIQA questions. It is not reasoning — it is defaulting to a positional heuristic. The fine-tuned guide's verbal plan forces engagement with the question content, reducing the A-selection rate to 42.1%. The 51.3 percentage-point suppression is the largest position bias correction documented in this study.

Why Baseline ECE Is So High
The baseline picks A on 93.4% of questions, so almost every vote goes the same way — 491 questions have Very High confidence (all 5 votes agree). But only 54.8% of those unanimous answers are correct. The model is simultaneously maximally confident and barely above chance. This is the worst possible calibration: certainty without accuracy. The guide breaks the A-bias, reducing false certainty and pushing accuracy up to 78.7% for the cases that remain high-confidence.
