"On open-ended arithmetic, the 3B model without fine-tuning doesn't just fail to help — it produces confident wrong answers that drag the ensemble far below the smaller model's baseline."
Why this matters for the paper's claim
The ablation argument (model size ruled out) is now supported by two independent datasets at N=300 each. The untuned 3B model underperforms the 1.5B baseline on both SVAMP (−9.6 pts) and ASDiv (−25.0 pts) at 2× the compute. This is not a statistical accident — it is a structural property of open-ended arithmetic generation in an untuned large model. The paper can now state this confidently as a replicated finding rather than a single observation.

The very-high confidence bucket in detail
At confidence ≥0.80 (5/5 or 4/5 votes agree), the ceiling model achieves only 29.6% accuracy — a gap of 60.4 percentage points from the expected 90%. This is the worst confidence–accuracy gap of any bucket across all conditions in this study. The untuned 3B model is generating wrong answers that all 5 votes unanimously agree on. Fine-tuned plan structure prevents this by forcing content-specific reasoning before the solver votes.
04Vote Distribution — Collapse Anatomy

168 of 300 questions (56%) receive 0/5 correct votes under the ceiling condition. Only 34 questions reach full 5/5 agreement on the correct answer. The distribution is heavily skewed toward the all-wrong pole — the opposite of a well-functioning ensemble.

All-wrong and high-agreement comparison
More all-wrong and fewer high-agree than baseline. Ensemble amplifies errors instead of correcting them.
Ensemble amplification of errors
Standard self-consistency voting improves accuracy because diverse sampling produces a correct answer more often than a wrong one. The untuned 3B model breaks this: on SVAMP, 5 passes of the 3B model produce a more wrong distribution than 5 passes of the 1.5B model. The ensemble amplifies the 3B model's systematic errors rather than averaging them out. 390 votes are wasted (votes not matching the final prediction) — 30% more than the guided pipeline's estimated ~103 wasted votes.
05Completing the Two-Pronged Elimination on SVAMP
Ablation argument — now fully grounded on SVAMP
Prong 1 (Model size ruled out): Untuned 3B×5 at 15B pp = 30.7% — 9.6 pts below the 1.5B baseline at 7.5B pp. More parameters, more compute, worse result.

Prong 2 (Prompting ruled out): CoT at 7.5B pp = 47.0% — better than the untuned 3B ceiling at 2× more compute. Even a simple "think step by step" prompt on the 1.5B model outperforms the full 3B model without fine-tuning.

Fine-tuned guided pipeline: 61.7% at 10.5B pp — 31 pts above ceiling, 21.4 pts above baseline, 14.7 pts above CoT.

Fine-tuning quality is the only condition that produces consistent positive results. This conclusion is now replicated independently across two arithmetic datasets (SVAMP and ASDiv).
