02 Why CoT is Sufficient for Science

ARC-Challenge tests science knowledge: biology, physics, chemistry, earth science. The 1.5B solver has been pre-trained on large corpora that include substantial science content. When you add a CoT prompt, you are not teaching the model science — you are prompting it to retrieve and apply what it already knows in a structured way.

"CoT works on ARC because the model already knows the science. The guide's structured plan adds nothing the model couldn't access with a step-by-step prompt."

Compare this to PIQA, where the 1.5B model collapses to option A even with CoT, gaining −0.6 pts. Physical intuition is not retrievable through prompting — the model lacks the embodied reasoning capability. The guide's structured plan bridges this gap. Science knowledge is retrievable. Physical intuition is not.

Consistency with the ablation
Your ablation already showed this pattern: the base 3B guide (no fine-tuning) added +2 pts on ARC through factual recall alone. Fine-tuning added +5 pts more. Now CoT shows that the 1.5B solver can access similar factual recall directly with a prompt — no 3B model needed at all. The science domain is exactly where Layer 1 (domain knowledge) dominates over Layer 2 (planning structure).

03 Compute Efficiency Implication

CoT achieves the same accuracy as the guided pipeline at 30% lower compute (7.5B vs 10.5B param-passes). For ARC-Challenge specifically, the guided pipeline is not the compute-optimal choice.

Honest assessment
A reviewer who reads this result will ask: "Why would anyone use the guided pipeline on ARC when CoT achieves the same result for less compute?" The correct answer is: they shouldn't. The guided pipeline is not universally better — it is specifically better on tasks where pretraining knowledge alone is insufficient. ARC is not one of those tasks. This is not a weakness of the paper; it is a precision boundary condition.

The key question this raises is: can you predict in advance which tasks need the guide? Your results suggest yes:

04 What to Change in the Paper
Must update — ARC framing
The paper currently presents ARC-Challenge as a confirmed guided pipeline result (+8.3 pts, ★★★★). This is still true — but it is incomplete. The paper must now disclose that CoT achieves the same +8.3 pts at lower compute. Without this disclosure, a reviewer who runs CoT on ARC will feel misled.
New framing — stronger claim
"On ARC-Challenge, both CoT prompting and the guided pipeline achieve 80.0% accuracy (+8.3 pts over baseline, p<0.0001). CoT is sufficient for science reasoning tasks where pretraining knowledge is retrievable through step-by-step prompting. The guided pipeline adds value specifically on tasks where pretraining knowledge alone is insufficient — physical reasoning (PIQA: CoT −0.6 pts vs guided +24.2 pts) and commonsense (CSQA: CoT +14 pts vs guided +22 pts)."
Practical contribution this creates
You now have a falsifiable, practical decision rule: use the guided pipeline when the task requires reasoning beyond what the model can retrieve through prompting; use CoT when domain knowledge is accessible but requires structuring. ARC-Challenge is the controlled negative that validates this rule.
CoT is sufficient for science. The guide is necessary for physical reasoning. This distinction is the paper's most practical contribution.
