01The Full Compute Picture

On ASDiv, the "ceiling" — running the untuned 3B model for all five votes at 15B parameter-passes — does not represent an upper bound. It is the worst-performing condition in the entire evaluation. The 3B model, without fine-tuning, generates open-ended arithmetic answers that are structurally inconsistent and numerically wrong at a high rate. More compute made things dramatically worse.

Critical finding
The ceiling does not function as a compute upper bound on open-ended arithmetic. The untuned 3B model generates plausible-looking but numerically incorrect answers with high confidence — 173 of 300 questions receive 0/5 correct votes, and 124 questions are answered at high confidence (≥0.80) but incorrectly. More parameter-passes means more confident wrongness.

02Why the 3B Model Collapses on Arithmetic

The 1.5B solver, despite being smaller, is prompted with a format that produces short, extractable numeric answers. The 3B model without fine-tuning produces longer, more verbose responses that often embed the answer inside a sentence rather than outputting it directly. On open-ended numeric tasks (ASDiv, SVAMP), this produces a high rate of format-extraction failures and numerically wrong confident answers.

"Fine-tuning does not just improve accuracy — it prevents the larger model from becoming a confident source of wrong answers."

The guided pipeline uses the fine-tuned 3B model for exactly one forward pass (the plan), then hands off to the 1.5B solver. The plan is not an answer — it is a structured decomposition that the 1.5B solver uses. This design avoids the 3B model's open-ended arithmetic generation failure mode entirely.

Paper claim strengthened
This result provides the strongest possible evidence for the paper's core claim. The ceiling result rules out the simplest alternative hypothesis: "just use the bigger model." At 15B parameter-passes — 43% more compute than the guided pipeline — the 3B model alone is worse than the 1.5B baseline by 25 points. Fine-tuning quality is not just the sufficient condition; it is what prevents catastrophic failure.
03Calibration Catastrophe

The ceiling's ECE of 0.444 is the worst calibration result in the entire study — worse than PIQA baseline (0.354), worse than SVAMP baseline (0.202). The high-confidence bucket tells the story: 176 of 300 questions reach ≥0.80 confidence, but those questions are only correct 29.5% of the time. The model is maximally confident and minimally accurate.

Division collapse
Division at 10.0% is particularly striking — worse than random for a multi-choice task and barely above chance for an open numeric task. The 3B model without fine-tuning systematically produces the wrong operand or operation for division problems, and votes converge unanimously on the wrong answer. The guided pipeline's plan step explicitly identifies the division operation and applies it to the correct operands — exactly the failure the curation process was designed to prevent.

05What This Means for the Paper
Ablation argument — now complete
The two-pronged elimination argument is now fully empirically grounded on ASDiv:

Prong 1 (model size ruled out): The untuned 3B model at ceiling compute (15B pp) scores 28.7% — 25 pts below the 1.5B baseline. Larger model = dramatically worse on arithmetic without fine-tuning.

Prong 2 (prompting ruled out): CoT on the 1.5B model scores 45.8% at equal baseline compute — better than the ceiling at 43% higher compute. The 3B model without fine-tuning cannot be rescued by prompting.

Fine-tuning quality is the only condition that produces positive results on ASDiv.
One honest disclosure needed
The paper must clearly state that the "ceiling" on ASDiv is not an upper bound — it is a baseline collapse. The claim "guided pipeline costs 30% less than the ceiling" implicitly treats the ceiling as desirable. On ASDiv, the ceiling is catastrophically undesirable. The paper should note: "On open-ended arithmetic, the ceiling condition (untuned 3B×5) collapses to 28.7%, 25 pts below baseline, confirming that compute-ceiling performance is task-dependent and not universally a practical upper bound."
