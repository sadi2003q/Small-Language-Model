02Why CoT Helps Arithmetic (But Not Enough)

SVAMP is an arithmetic word problem dataset. The 1.5B solver has been exposed to extensive arithmetic reasoning in its pretraining data, including many CoT-style worked examples. When you add "Let's think step by step", you activate this latent capability — the model can now retrieve and apply simple arithmetic reasoning that it knows but fails to use without prompting.

This explains the +10.3 pt CoT gain. It is real — arithmetic is a domain where step-by-step prompting has something concrete to activate. Compare to PIQA, where CoT gives −0.6 pts because physical intuition is not retrievable through prompting.

"CoT activates arithmetic reasoning the model already has. The guide provides arithmetic reasoning the model didn't know it needed for this specific problem."

The +14.7 pt gap between CoT and the guided pipeline tells you something more specific. The fine-tuned guide reads the problem, identifies the operation type (addition, subtraction, division), isolates the relevant numbers, and generates a step-by-step plan before the solver votes. This is not just "think step by step" — it is problem-specific structured decomposition. CoT gives the solver a general instruction; the guide gives the solver a problem-specific plan.

The key difference
CoT prompt: "Let's think step by step." — generic instruction, model applies it with varying quality.

Guide output: "Step 1: Identify operation — subtraction. Step 2: First value = 17. Step 3: Second value = 14. Step 4: Compute 17 − 14 = 3." — problem-specific, forces content engagement. The solver now votes on a structured decomposition, not a vague instruction to reason.

CoT improves calibration by 26.5% over baseline. The guided pipeline improves it by a further 53.8% over CoT. The three levels are not overlapping — each step represents a qualitatively different confidence regime. This monotonic ladder is the strongest calibration finding across all four CoT experiments.

Why the ladder is clean on SVAMP
Arithmetic has clear right/wrong answers with no ambiguity. When the solver votes correctly, all 5 votes converge (high confidence, high accuracy). When it fails, votes scatter. CoT partially reduces scatter by activating step-by-step reasoning. The guide almost eliminates it by providing the correct decomposition — when the plan is right, all 5 solver votes agree. This is why SVAMP has the highest vote consistency lift (1.90×) in the entire evaluation.

The decision rule this creates
The four results together establish a practical decision rule for when to use the guided pipeline:

Use CoT only when the domain is factual/scientific and the model has strong pretraining coverage (ARC). Cheaper, same result.

Use the guided pipeline when: (1) the task requires physical intuition unavailable through prompting (PIQA), or (2) the task requires structured decomposition that CoT only partially activates (SVAMP, CSQA). The guide adds +14–23 pts over CoT in these cases.

SVAMP and CSQA are the datasets where the guide earns its 40% compute premium most clearly — CoT is not sufficient, but CoT shows the potential the guide then fully realises.
05One Flag — Reduced Votes per Question
Technical note for the paper
The average votes per CoT question is 3.2 out of 5 — meaning approximately 32 questions received fewer than 5 parseable answers (10.7% empty rate). The baseline has 7.7% empty rate. Both are higher than the guided pipeline's near-zero extraction failure rate.

This means CoT accuracy of 47.0% is slightly underestimated — the missing votes are counted as wrong by default. If those 32 questions had full 5-vote ensembles, CoT accuracy would likely be 1–2 pts higher (~48–49%). This doesn't change the qualitative story but should be noted in the paper's limitations or experimental details section.
06What to Write in the Paper
Update needed
The paper currently presents SVAMP as a purely guided pipeline result without CoT comparison. Add the three-way comparison explicitly in the Analysis section alongside PIQA and ARC-Challenge comparisons.
Suggested text for §V (Main Results) or §VI (Analysis)
"On arithmetic tasks (SVAMP), CoT prompting achieves +10.3 pts over baseline at equal compute (7.5B param-passes), confirming that step-by-step reasoning is partially accessible through prompting on this domain. The guided pipeline adds a further +14.7 pts over CoT (total +25.0 pts), demonstrating that the fine-tuned guide's problem-specific structured decomposition substantially exceeds what prompt engineering alone achieves. This contrasts with ARC-Challenge, where CoT and the guided pipeline produce identical results, and with PIQA, where CoT produces no gain while the guide delivers +23.8 pts — confirming that the guide's value is highest precisely where pretraining knowledge is insufficient to support step-by-step prompting alone."
CoT activates what's already there. The guide builds what isn't. SVAMP needs both — and the guide builds more.
