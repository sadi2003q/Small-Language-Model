⚠ CATASTROPHIC

CoT vs Baseline Gain by Operation Type

The aggregate −2.67 is entirely driven by the Multiplication collapse. Without it, CoT would be slightly positive overall.

All Three Conditions by Operation

Pipeline beats CoT on every operation type. CoT beats baseline only on Division and Multi-step.

Multiplication Collapse — Why This Happens

Multiplication at 65.1% baseline → 41.9% CoT is a 23.3 pt collapse driven by one mechanism: the 1.5B model generates plausible intermediate multiplication steps that contain arithmetic errors (e.g. computing 7×8=54 instead of 56), then confidently presents the wrong result as the answer. The baseline avoids this because it does not try to show working — it pattern-matches to a direct answer. The CoT's explicit reasoning introduces a new failure mode that the baseline was immune to. This is the "confident misdirection" pattern, but triggered by format rather than by an untuned guide.

Division Benefit — Why CoT Helps Here

Division improves from 27.5% to 37.5% with CoT (+10 pts). Division problems have a clear procedural structure (divide numerator by denominator), so even a 1.5B model's step-by-step chain reliably identifies the right operation and executes it. The problem is simple enough that the chain cannot go wrong in complex ways. This aligns with the general principle: CoT helps when the reasoning procedure is short and unambiguous; it hurts when intermediate steps require precise arithmetic that a small model miscalculates.

What This Means for Your Paper

The aggregate result (−2.67 pts) is the headline for the paper, but the operation-type breakdown is the scientific contribution. You now have direct evidence that CoT at 1.5B scale is not just ineffective — it is selectively harmful, and the pattern predicts where it will fail.

The paper's argument is now three-level: (1) Raw majority voting is a reasonable baseline. (2) CoT prompt engineering at the same compute fails on average and catastrophically on multiplication. (3) Fine-tuned guided planning at +40% compute solves all operation types, including the ones CoT makes worse. This is a stronger argument than "our pipeline beats CoT" — it shows why CoT fails and why fine-tuning is necessary.

The calibration result is the clearest finding: Both CoT and baseline show ECE ~0.30, confirming that prompt engineering alone cannot fix the 1.5B model's arithmetic calibration problem. The pipeline's 0.100 ECE shows this is solvable — but only through fine-tuned external guidance, not self-generated reasoning chains.

Combined with the CSQA result, you now have a consistent cross-domain story: CoT at 1.5B hurts on commonsense (−2.78 pts, E-bias amplified) and hurts on arithmetic (−2.67 pts, multiplication collapsed). The fine-tuned pipeline outperforms CoT by +8.1 pts on CSQA and ~+19.7 pts on ASDiv. CoT is not the alternative — it is a demonstration of why fine-tuning is needed.
