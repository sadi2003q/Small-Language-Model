The Core Paradox
CoT reduces the position bias score from 0.424 to 0.058 — an 86% reduction. The baseline selects sol1 on 92.4% of questions; CoT brings this to 44.2%, near the expected 50%. Yet accuracy moves from 55.4% to 54.8%. Bias correction and accuracy improvement are completely decoupled.

Why CoT accuracy didn't improve
The baseline was scoring 55.4% because it collapsed to Sol1 — and Sol1 happened to be correct on ~53% of questions. Once CoT redistributes votes uniformly, that accidental accuracy advantage from the bias disappears. What's left is the model's genuine ability to reason about physical tasks without a guide — which is approximately 55%. The bias was masking real incompetence, not hiding real ability.
03What the Guide Does Differently
"CoT tells the model to think step by step. The fine-tuned guide tells the model what steps to take — with the specific physical principle applied to this specific question."

Both CoT and the guided pipeline fix the positional collapse. The difference is what happens after the bias is removed. When CoT redistributes votes, those votes are now scattered based on shallow reasoning — the 1.5B model lacks the physical intuition to choose correctly. Vote consistency lift is only 1.013× (essentially nothing).

The fine-tuned guide provides a structured plan that anchors the solver to the physical content of the question. The solver is no longer guessing — it is executing a plan. This is why high-agreement correct questions increase dramatically under guidance.

What CoT provides

Instruction to reason slowly. Redistributes votes away from positional heuristics. Does not supply physical domain knowledge. Result: votes now uniform but still wrong — the model guesses uniformly instead of collapsing uniformly.

What the guide provides

A structured plan identifying the physical principle and applying it to the specific quantities in the question. Solver votes are now anchored to content. Result: votes converge on correct answers because the reasoning path is correct.

Vote consistency

CoT lift: 1.013× (flat). Guided lift: 1.43×. The guided pipeline produces 43% more correct votes per question — not because it adds diversity, but because each individual vote is better informed.

Wasted votes

Baseline: 70 wasted (because votes are unanimous — unanimously wrong). CoT: 347 wasted (votes now spread, but split incorrectly). This is the quantitative signature of uniform-but-wrong voting — high waste, low accuracy.

04Implications for the Paper
Correction required
The current paper states: "This single bias correction accounts for a substantial fraction of the +24.2 pt accuracy gain." This is now proven incorrect by the CoT data. CoT eliminates the bias and produces −0.6 pts. The +24.2 pt gain is therefore not attributable to bias correction — it reflects pure reasoning quality improvement. This sentence must be rewritten before submission.
New correct framing
"CoT prompting eliminates the A-option collapse (92.4% → 44.2%, bias score 0.424 → 0.058) at equal compute but produces no accuracy gain (−0.6 pts). The guided pipeline's +24.2 pt gain is therefore not attributable to bias suppression — it reflects genuine improvement in physical reasoning quality enabled by structured verbal planning. Bias correction is a byproduct of content-anchored guidance, not its mechanism."
New contribution this adds
This result enables a clean three-way mechanistic separation: (1) position bias is eliminated by content-grounding — CoT achieves this; (2) reasoning quality is improved by structured planning — only the fine-tuned guide achieves this. The CoT experiment turns what was a confounded result into a controlled decomposition. This is a stronger paper than before.
