⚠️ The Mechanism: CoT Amplifies E-Option Confusion
When the 1.5B solver is asked to "think step by step," it generates a reasoning attempt that frequently lands on option E. E-selection rises from 21.4% (baseline) to 29.5% (CoT) — a 37% relative increase above the expected 20%. This reasoning-induced bias, combined with low accuracy when picking E (50.5% vs baseline E-accuracy 64.6%), explains the −3.0 pt drop. CoT doesn't help the model reason — it gives it a scripted path to the wrong answer.

DEFINITIVE CONCLUSION — CSQA CEILING EXPERIMENT
"CommonsenseQA reveals a nuanced picture that contrasts sharply with ARC-Challenge. The untuned 3B ceiling achieves 75.0% — near-parity with the guided pipeline (75.8%) — because commonsense world-knowledge is partially accessible through pretraining scale. The ceiling's ECE (0.107) is even better than the pipeline's (0.141), the lowest false-confidence count (138), and the lowest all-wrong rate (114). Yet despite leading on calibration, the ceiling loses on accuracy, consistency, and E-option bias. Most critically: CoT collapses (67.7%), worsening both accuracy and calibration against baseline — confirming that unstructured step-by-step reasoning actively introduces failure modes that fine-tuned verbal planning prevents."

📄 Paper Implication
CSQA modifies the three-tier taxonomy. It sits between ARC (Tier 2 — domain-accessible) and PIQA/SVAMP (Tier 3 — guide necessary). The ceiling nearly replicates the pipeline on accuracy but falls on consistency and bias suppression. Recommended: introduce a Tier 2.5 — "Partially accessible" — where scale helps but fine-tuned verbal planning still adds marginal gains across multiple dimensions. Add CSQA ceiling to ablation Table III with the note that ECE improvement is ceiling-led but accuracy improvement remains pipeline-led.
